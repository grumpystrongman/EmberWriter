# The Author-Led Book Studio

## Product promise

**The author directs. EmberWriter develops, drafts, challenges and produces.**

EmberWriter is first and foremost a creative writing studio. Authors can write every
line by hand, invite AI to help with a scene, or give the studio enough detailed
material to draft an entire novel. These are three levels of the **same** workspace,
not competing products.

The author remains the source of canon, voice, creative choices, accepted edits,
and publication decisions. Every AI-generated manuscript chapter is a candidate,
never an automatic replacement. When a draft is wrong, the author can rewrite it,
reject it, or change the next chapter's direction.

## What is built in this delivery

- **Book Studio** (Plan → Book Studio): author-provided premise, voice and chapter
  blueprint, or a proposed outline generated by a locally installed Ollama model.
  The author can edit the proposed outline before saving it. Up to 80 chapters;
  target 300–5,000 words per chapter.
- **Write the whole book**: author-started, server-side sequential generation
  with a persisted job record, progress, pause-after-current-chapter, explicit
  resume, and restart recovery for jobs that were queued or actively running.
  The browser can close while a chapter is generated. A paused job remains
  paused across service restarts. Each finished chapter is an unapproved
  candidate for editorial review, and no existing manuscript is overwritten.
  Each chapter uses its own requirements, previous chapter and available canon;
  a local job stops for author attention when generation fails.
- **Independent Reader Panel**: for each draft candidate, optionally call three
  separate local AI perspectives (Genre Fan, Developmental Editor, Line Editor).
  Readers score five craft dimensions and cite exact candidate text for each
  actionable finding. Findings that cannot be verified against the candidate
  are withheld. The author accepts or dismisses each finding and can build an
  accepted-only revision brief; no rewrite is applied automatically. Editing
  a draft marks older reviews stale. Each reviewer is evaluated independently
  rather than receiving other models' feedback.
- **Manuscript Intelligence** (Plan → Manuscript Intelligence): bounded, offline
  project-scoped scan of existing chapters and unapproved Book Studio candidates.
  The dashboard includes chapter length pulse, sentence statistics, counts of
  mechanical writing flags, exact phrase/name presence, repeated four-word
  echoes across chapters, and an author-owned foreshadowing/story-promise ledger
  with source evidence. Untouched starter documents are excluded; approved Book
  Studio chapters are counted exactly once. The author must explicitly resolve,
  reopen, or abandon plot threads: a literal mention is never taken as proof of
  fulfillment, nor is character presence interpreted as character agency.
- **Revision Workbench** (Book Studio, beneath reader panels): compose an author-led
  rewrite from approved reader findings and/or explicit author direction. Local
  Ollama drafts a complete replacement **proposal**, leaving the original
  candidate untouched until the author reviews a side-by-side comparison and
  accepts. Source hashes invalidate outdated proposals, and acceptance invokes
  Book Studio's archived candidate revision mechanism. Rejected revisions do
  not modify the manuscript. Mechanical quality indicators and word/line
  counts appear before and after; they are not a literary quality score.
- **Editorial control**: read/edit/reject/approve candidates. Candidate revisions
  are archived locally. Only approval writes to a manuscript file under
  `manuscript/book-studio/`. Existing files are never overwritten. The Binder
  is synced so approved chapters become visible in the manuscript workflow.
  Unwritten and rejected chapter briefs can be redirected independently.
- **Canon Ripple** (Autopilot → Canon Ripple): author proposes a canon change;
  locally scan project references, approve or reject the proposal, track per-file
  reviews, re-scan and resolve. The scan is literal text matching and does not
  claim to understand semantic contradictions; manuscript/canon is never edited.
- **Quality Clinic** (Autopilot → Quality Clinic): per-chapter local metrics and
  anchored findings for repeats, spacing, excessive punctuation, generic AI-like
  phrasing, weak phrases, repeated sentence openings and possible passive voice.
  These are transparent mechanical hints, not a professional grammar checker.
- **Offline Audiobook Workshop** (Publish → Audiobook Workshop): select an existing
  chapter, choose a Windows SAPI system voice and synthesize a narrated WAV
  locally. FFmpeg converts to MP3 when available. Listen and download individual
  tracks or a ZIP with chapter audio and manifest. No hosted TTS calls. This is
  a functional first narration pass, not a mastered/multi-voice M4B package.
- **Existing publishing stack**: EPUB/DOCX/PDF compilation, Cover Studio, release
  validation, preview, distribution, and submission tools remain in place.

## Priorities for complete AutoNovel parity and beyond

1. **Next production-conductor upgrades**: configurable time/token budgets,
   resource-aware scheduling, chapter-by-chapter acceptance gates during
   unattended drafts, retry policies, multiple simultaneous book queues with
   resource limits, and robust supervision for long-running local models.
2. **Long-book memory and revision intelligence**: automatic canon proposals
   after each chapter, dependency/ripple graph, named beat coverage, character
   state diffs, foreshadowing ledger, repeated motifs and semantic contradictions
   with evidence/uncertainty.
3. **Advanced grammar + craft analysis**: optional offline LanguageTool or local
   proofing engine, mechanical and model rubrics with real passages as evidence,
   pacing graphs, dialogue voice separation, slop phrase patterns, word echoes,
   tense/POV drift, repetition by chapter, cut proposals, accepted/ignored
   findings and blind A/B chapter experiments.
4. **Independent reader and editor panels**: multiple local model roles, diverse
   genre-reader perspectives, critic/professor review, ranked revision briefs,
   plateau detection, side-by-side draft comparison, explicit approval and undo.
5. **Professional audiobook suite**: local natural-voice engines (Piper/Kokoro or
   similarly licensed offline voices), speaker-attributed dialogue, character
   casting, pronunciation dictionary, chapter/master loudness, audio chapters,
   MP3/M4B export, cover metadata and timing preview.
6. **Retail publishing polish**: print-accurate interior/bleed/trim, e-book
   accessibility, ISBN/imprint metadata, proofing checks, retailer previews,
   embedded fonts as permitted, release packages and a static landing page.

None of these future stages should silently upload manuscript content, force
a cloud subscription, or make author approval optional where text is replaced.

## Privacy boundary

Only synthetic project data is used for automated tests. Book Studio rejects
external-generation provider URLs; it explicitly requires a loopback Ollama
endpoint. Offline Audiobook uses installed Windows speech voices; output audio
is stored under `<project>/.ember/audiobook/`. Demo and Private server instances
continue to use distinct storage roots. Existing manuscript files and personal
databases are not committed.

**Important nuance**: other pre-existing EmberWriter workspaces can be configured
to use remote-compatible providers; this feature's local-only policy does not
certify all historical parts of the application against outbound traffic.

## Positioning copy

**You write the story—or direct EmberWriter as it writes it with you.**

Start from an idea, a meticulously planned world, a finished outline, or a
half-written manuscript. Choose how much creative work to delegate. Draft the
entire book if you want, then step inside any chapter to adjust its voice,
structure, stakes, relationships or ending. Your universe remains yours.

An intelligent writing partner should give the author more possibilities, more
control, and more time for the storytelling choices that matter.


## Story-level editorial intelligence (November 2026)

These three workspaces extend the author-led flow beyond chapter drafting:

- **Story Arc Investigator** (Plan -> Story Arc Investigator): an explicitly
  requested local AI assessment of one chapter at a time. It flags plausible
  on-page decisions, causal consequences, relationship changes, revelations,
  conflicts, and foreshadowing hypotheses. Every finding must quote a passage
  verified against the examined draft; unsupported claims are rejected. The
  author accepts, dismisses, or promotes foreshadowing hypotheses to the
  Story Promise Ledger. After source edits, older findings are marked stale
  and cannot be acted on. A deterministic whole-book Arc Dossier rolls up
  accepted findings into subject trajectories and subjective tension trend,
  and can be exported as Markdown. The dossier is not an independent proof
  of narrative causality, coherence, or literary quality.
- **Voiceprint Lab** (Plan -> Voiceprint Lab): choose a long sample of your
  actual writing in the current project (at least 120 words). A deterministic
  offline comparison shows sentence length, sentence-length variation,
  paragraph length, dialogue density, punctuation rates, and deviations
  across authored chapters and draft candidates. No model calls and no
  rewrites. Results are descriptive, not author-identity, AI-generation,
  plagiarism, or writing-quality scores.
- **Production Readiness Gate** (Publish -> Production Readiness Gate):
  advisory local preflight for incomplete chapter plans, unapproved drafts,
  outstanding canon changes, open story promises, possible proofreading
  errors, editorial placeholders, Binder compile coverage, and release
  metadata. The author may waive a warning only with a written rationale.
  Waivers are source-fingerprint-aware for content warnings and do not
  silently carry to newly edited prose. Blockers are never waived, but
  the existing publishing tools are not disabled. The report is exportable
  as Markdown. A clean preflight is NOT retailer or professional certification.

All three workspaces use project-local data and deliberately do not read
other project libraries. The only model work here is the opt-in narrative
arc assessment, which requires a locally bound Ollama provider. These tools
were tested with fictional synthetic stories.


### Whole-book offline narration queue

Audiobook Workshop now has a separate **Narrate entire book** action. It reads
the author's selected Binder Draft documents in actual compile order, captures
content fingerprints, and creates one local Windows SAPI track at a time. The
progress record lives in the selected project's `.ember/audiobook-batch.json`.
The author can pause after the current chapter and resume later; queued/running
batches recover after service restart, while paused work remains paused.

A chapter edited after the batch began triggers **needs attention**, rather than
silently creating audio from changed prose. A new batch can be started after the
author reviews changes. A completed batch exports a numbered, ordered ZIP with
chapter WAV/MP3 tracks and checksums, without mixing in older narration tracks.

This is a functional single-voice, locally hosted chapter-narration pipeline,
**not yet a production-mastered audiobook**. Offline natural voices, character
casting, pronunciation controls, loudness mastering, M4B and retailer-validated
distribution remain planned. On Windows, an interrupted text-to-speech subprocess
may still finish briefly during shutdown; the job's persisted state allows
checking its output after restart. Per-project rendering is serialized to avoid
concurrent manifest corruption.


### Author-managed Style Bible and terminology audit

Use **Plan -> Style Bible** to define three categories of project-local rules:
preferred spelling (flag the alternate and display the preferred form),
avoid phrases (repetition, filler, or stylistic habits the author wishes to
review), and watch expressions (names and invented terminology). Rules support
case-sensitive matching and optional author guidance. The scan covers current
manuscript chapters plus Book Studio candidates using bounded, whole-word or
whole-phrase exact matching. Each finding cites source chapter, line number,
and excerpt.

Authors can dismiss a specific occurrence with a written reason, or reopen
it for review. Dismissals depend on a source fingerprint and expire after
chapter edits. The editor never replaces text automatically. The **Production
Readiness Gate** now reports outstanding Style Bible findings so they can be
reviewed before publishing; dismissing the occurrence in Style Bible clears
that particular preflight warning. A preflight waiver is advisory only.

This is deterministic terminology checking, **not a full grammar checker**;
dictionary-backed local grammar, character-aware language, and semantic
consistency analysis remain separate roadmap milestones.
